EU–Kenya: AI Factories show that sovereignty needs an evidence chain

Article created November 25, 2026 · Announcement analysed November 24, 2026 · Source: European Commission

The EU–Kenya Digital Dialogue, held in Brussels on November 23 and 24, launched a digital-cooperation roadmap. The European Commission links AI Factories to discussions on trusted networks, data centres and sovereign cloud infrastructure. For a Belgian or French business, the useful signal is less diplomatic than methodological: autonomy has to be built across the AI operating chain.

1. An AI Factory is not a sovereignty qualification

Compute capacity, an innovation programme or a local data centre can broaden deployment options. On their own, however, they do not prove where data travels, who can administer environments, which subcontractors are involved, or how records are retained. An architecture may be European while retaining technical, contractual or operational dependencies that need to be mapped.

2. Turn infrastructure into verifiable controls

Before a RAG pilot, document assistant or agent connected to Odoo Enterprise, request a flow map covering input data, indexes, logs, backups, telemetry and support. For each flow, record its region, retention period, owner and contractual basis. Then verify administrator identities, maintenance access, encryption, log export and the revocation mechanism.

3. Retain an exit capability

Operational sovereignty means an organisation can take control back. Test export of corpora and metadata, recovery of configurations, portability of prompts and evaluations, and replacement of a model or inference component. These exercises should be performed on a realistic scope, rather than merely described in a contract.

4. What this changes for Belgian and French organisations

The announced international cooperation creates neither an obligation nor a certification for European organisations. It does, however, underline that AI Factories, cloud and data centres are components of a broader industrial policy. For an SME, enterprise or public body, the purchasing criterion should remain the ability to produce evidence: effective location, access rights, logs, responsibilities, continuity and reversibility. This documentation makes it possible to compare private, hybrid and managed offerings without mistaking geographical proximity for actual control.

Underside recommendation: make the evidence chain — flows, identities, operations, records and exit — an acceptance deliverable before describing an AI platform as sovereign.
