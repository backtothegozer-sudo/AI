Sovereign AI: building durable capability without locking in choices

Article created on October 13, 2026 · Publication analysed: October 12, 2026 · Source: Arm

Arm defines sovereign AI as the ability to develop, deploy, and govern a trusted ecosystem in line with national priorities. Its framework—ecosystem, partner choice, proven foundation, scalable compute, and energy efficiency—is useful when converted into evidence rather than treated as a technology promise.

1. Five principles, several layers of dependency

Arm highlights a collaborative ecosystem, partner flexibility, a proven global foundation, scalable compute, and energy efficiency. It says more than 350 billion Arm-based chips have shipped and more than 22 million developers build on its platform. These figures describe ecosystem scale; they do not establish the sovereignty of any specific project. Silicon, firmware, systems, models, data, operations, support, and legal rights must each be assessed.

2. What changes for a Belgian or French organisation

A company or administration should assess its ability to continue, move, and audit an AI service—not only where it is hosted. For RAG, an agent, or Odoo automation, that means exportable formats, documented interfaces, controlled identities, retrievable logs, and a fallback option. European infrastructure may reduce some risks, but continuity also depends on licences, components, updates, skills, and supply chains.

3. Underside analysis: preserve choice through architecture

Choice is not created at contract signature; it is built with replaceable layers. Separate data, RAG indexes, models, orchestration, and business connectors; test at least one model substitution; keep authorisation policies outside the model; and document hardware and software dependencies. For Odoo and agents, business writes should remain governed by permissions, approvals, and traces independent from the inference provider.

4. Energy, skills, and useful capacity

Arm notes that power, cooling, space, and operating costs constrain national capacity. The useful organisational metric is therefore sustained business workload within an energy and financial envelope, not theoretical peak performance. Skills are the other durable asset: operations, cybersecurity, data governance, model evaluation, and recovery must be exercisable locally even when some components come from a global ecosystem.

Operational recommendation: turn the five principles into an evidence matrix for each workload: substitutable suppliers, export formats, critical dependencies, power use, available skills, support access, offline scenario, and recovery time. Test a model or component change annually and retain the results.
