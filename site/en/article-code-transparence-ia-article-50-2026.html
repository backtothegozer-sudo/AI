AI Act: signing the transparency code becomes an execution issue

Article created on 22 June 2026 · Publication analyzed: 10 June 2026 · Source: European Commission

Share inLinkedIn XX fFacebook

The European Commission's 10 June 2026 publication on signing the code of practice for transparency of AI-generated content changes the conversation: Article 50 AI Act compliance is now an immediate execution workstream. The 22 June 2026 info session and the 2 August 2026 applicability date give companies a concrete delivery schedule.

1. What the Commission is actually saying

The European Commission is inviting providers and deployers of generative systems to sign the code of practice on transparency of AI-generated content. The benefit is explicit: signatories can rely on an EU-recognized framework, with more predictability, lower administrative burden, and a more consistent interpretation across national authorities.

The critical point is timing. Article 50 marking and labeling obligations start applying on 2 August 2026. In other words, this is no longer a theoretical compliance topic. Teams now need to decide how AI interactions will be disclosed, how certain generated or manipulated content will be marked, and how implementation evidence will be retained.

2. Why this matters for AI Belgium and AI France programs

This regulatory sequence is directly actionable for AI Belgium and AI France initiatives. The real issue is not only legal. It is operational. Teams need to know which flows generate text, images, or voice, which interfaces expose users to AI content, and which processes must retain compliance evidence.

Organizations that move early gain on three fronts: lower regulatory risk, better editorial quality, and stronger governance for automations. This is also an SEO topic. Better-governed AI content, with clearer review paths and traceability, reduces the drift that ultimately harms trust, brand consistency, and search performance.

3. Operational reading for Odoo Belgium, Odoo France, and Odoo Enterprise

In Odoo Belgium, Odoo France, or broader Odoo Enterprise environments, this development directly affects chatbots, product-page generation, support replies, CRM summaries, sales assistants, document workflows, and marketing automations. Every journey needs to be reviewed through one simple question: at which point must a human be informed that they are interacting with AI or reading AI-generated content?

The right move is to create a use-case map: public content, internal content, evidentiary content, and human-assisted content. That segmentation helps define disclosures, approval rules, logging zones, and remediation priorities without unnecessarily slowing down business execution.

Run an "Article 50 AI Act + Odoo workflows" audit to identify visible AI content, disclosure duties, and evidence requirements before 2 August 2026.
